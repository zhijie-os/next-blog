## Motivation

On-policy distillation (OPD) is substantially more training-efficient than RL-based post-training methods such as PPO, GRPO, and DAPO.

The usual explanation is that OPD provides denser and more stable supervision. This paper instead studies the problem from the perspective of **parameter dynamics**:

> Why does OPD obtain larger reasoning improvements with smaller parameter changes?

The authors argue that OPD develops a form of **foresight** early in training: it quickly discovers both **where parameters should change** and **which directions they should move in**.

## 1. Module-Allocation Foresight

OPD and RL do not distribute their parameter updates equally across the network.

By injecting local trained updates back into the base model, the authors measure how much reasoning performance is recovered by updates from different layers and module types.

The main observations are:

- MLP updates contribute more to reasoning performance than attention updates.
- Middle-layer MLPs are particularly important.
- RL makes relatively large updates even in regions with low marginal utility.
- OPD concentrates more of its update budget on functionally useful regions.

Thus, OPD is more selective about **where** it changes the model.

## 2. Early Low-Rank Lock-in

The authors then analyze the geometry of the parameter updates using SVD.

OPD updates are more strongly concentrated in a small number of dominant singular directions than RL updates. More importantly, the dominant subspace found early in OPD training is already strongly aligned with the final update subspace.

Therefore, later OPD training appears to mostly continue moving within directions that were discovered early.

This gives the second form of foresight:

> OPD discovers not only where to update, but also the useful **update directions** early in training.

Norm-scaling experiments further show that scaling an early OPD update along these already-established directions can recover a large fraction of the later model's performance. This suggests that early checkpoints are often missing **update magnitude**, rather than fundamentally missing the correct directions.

## EffOPD

Based on this observation, the authors propose **EffOPD**: instead of waiting for normal OPD training to gradually move farther along an already-stable direction, extrapolate along that direction.

EffOPD performs extrapolation at exponentially spaced checkpoints

$$
t = 2^n.
$$

At each checkpoint, the recent parameter displacement is used as an estimate of the future update direction:

$$
\Delta_n = W_{2^n} - W_{2^{n-1}}.
$$

For the first checkpoint,

$$
\Delta_0 = W_1 - W_0.
$$

EffOPD generates five increasingly aggressive extrapolation candidates:

$$
\widetilde{W}_{n,k}
=
W_{2^n} + 2k\Delta_n,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,5.
$$

A small validation set of 50 examples is used to evaluate the candidates sequentially.

1. Start with the current OPD checkpoint as the accepted model.
2. Test the candidate with $k=1$.
3. If validation performance improves or stays equal, accept it and test $k=2$.
4. Continue increasing $k$ while performance improves.
5. Stop immediately when a candidate performs worse.
6. Continue training from the best accepted parameters.

If even $k=1$ fails, EffOPD performs no extrapolation and reduces to ordinary OPD.

The idea is therefore very simple:

$$
\text{stable early direction}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{move farther along that direction}.
$$

The validation check prevents the algorithm from extrapolating too far when the local direction is no longer predictive.

## Takeaway

The main contribution of this paper is not a complicated new training objective. It provides an empirical explanation for why OPD is efficient:

- **Module-level foresight:** OPD concentrates updates on regions that are useful for reasoning.
- **Direction-level foresight:** its dominant low-rank update directions stabilize early and align with the final solution.

EffOPD directly exploits the second property through parameter extrapolation and reports roughly **3× training acceleration** while maintaining comparable final performance.
